# The CHEETAH Email Framework

Created by Pierce Ujjainwalla, Co-Founder & CEO of Knak. Full guide:
**<https://knak.com/resources/guides/cheetah/?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=skill&utm_campaign=claude-plugin>**

Seven checks that take an email from *"meh"* to *"amehzing"*, plus one top tip that
matters more than any of them.

Use this file two ways:

- **When writing** — every line of copy has to earn its place against these seven.
- **When scoring** — each letter becomes a graded criterion (see the rubric at the bottom).

---

## C — CAPTIVATE

> "You only get one shot, one opportunity."

The inbox is the whole battle. Three things decide whether the email gets opened:
**sender display name, subject line, preview text.**

Rules:

- **Sender display name** should be clear, memorable and relatable. **It performs better as a person** than as a company — "Pierce Ujjainwalla" beats "Knak Marketing Team". A person + company hybrid ("Pierce at Knak") is the safe middle ground.
- **Subject line must be actionable.** Verb-led, specific, and about something the reader can do or get.
- **Preview text adds context to the subject line** — it does not repeat it. Treat them as a two-line pair that only makes sense together.
- **Keep preview text under 100 characters.**
- **Consider emojis** in the subject line to catch the eye. One, placed deliberately.
- **Use clear, simple language** that doesn't put the reader off.

> "Opens are half the battle. Success is the only option."

Worked example from the guide:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| From | A named person at your company, e.g. Jordan Reyes <jordan.reyes@example.com> |
| Reply-To | The same person's real, monitored address |
| Subject | The 5 things to boost open rates ✨ |
| Preview text | And the 3 things you should never do. |

Note what the preview text does: it *extends* the subject into a second promise
rather than restating it.

## H — HUMAN

> "Are you writing in authentic language like a human?"

Test: **does this feel like it came from a person, or from a big faceless company?**

- **The 1-person email trick.** Pick one real person you know on your list. Write the whole email as if you're sending them a 1-to-1 message. Then send it to everyone. This single technique kills more corporate jargon than any style guide.
- Cut jargon, cut the corporate "we are pleased to announce" register, cut anything you wouldn't say out loud.
- Talk about the reader's benefit, not the sender's accomplishment.

---

## E — ENTERTAIN

> "Who said B2B has to be boring?"

- **Does it make someone laugh?**
- **Edu-tainment is effective** — teach something *and* be enjoyable about it.
- **Take some risks.** The guide's flagship example is a Salesforce subject line, *"Is your data \*\*cked up?"*, where the asterisks are revealed by an animated GIF to be innocent. It stands out precisely because it's unexpected from that sender.

Calibrate risk to brand and audience. The point is not to be edgy; it's to not be forgettable.

---

## E — EASY

> "Define the GOAL — what do you want them to do?"

- **One goal. One Call-to-Action.** Choose a single CTA and focus everything on it.
- Repeat that same CTA (top and bottom is the usual pattern) rather than offering competing actions.
- **Make it easy to read** — short sentences, scannable structure, numbered next steps, plenty of white space. The guide points at [readable.com](https://readable.com) for a readability score.
- The CTA links to an on-brand campaign landing page, not a generic homepage.

---

## T — TRANSITIONS

> "Get creative with the transitions in your emails."

- **No one said every section needed a straight line across it.** A flat 1px horizontal rule between blocks is the default nobody chose.
- Curved dividers, angled section breaks, torn-paper edges, overlapping shapes, a colour block that runs behind two sections.
- **"It's just another image to add!"** — a transition is a design asset, so it belongs in the brief alongside the hero image.

---

## A — ANIMATE

> "Helps to stand out, be different — catch attention."

- **Be subtle.** Animation should reward attention, not fight it. Nothing blinking or looping aggressively.
- Animated GIFs can carry a reveal (see the Salesforce example under ENTERTAIN) or simply add life to a hero image.
- An **animated sender profile picture** stands out in a mobile inbox list — a CAPTIVATE trick as much as an ANIMATE one.
- Build GIFs with [EZGIF.com](https://ezgif.com) — **and watch the file size.**
- Always specify a meaningful first frame: many clients and dark-mode setups show a static frame only.

---

## H — HARMONY

> "Are your emails and landing pages consistent?"

- **Your email should hook and your landing page should convert.** Two jobs, one continuous experience.
- The headline, offer, imagery and language on the landing page should visibly continue the email — not contradict it, not restate it in different words.
- **Do you make it easy for your prospects to convert?** Check the form length, the number of fields, and that the promise made in the email is the promise fulfilled on the page.

---

## TOP TIP — VALUABLE

> "The most important thing that is often overlooked."
>
> "No one cares about your webinar, your website, your blog. They care about what's
> in it for them. **So make it valuable to them.**"

Every claim in the email should pass the *"and therefore, for you…"* test. If a
sentence is about the sender, rewrite it or cut it.

---

## Scoring rubric

Score each criterion **Strong / Adequate / Weak / Missing** and give one concrete,
rewritable reason. Never score without naming the fix.

| Criterion | Score against |
|---|---|
| **Captivate** | Sender name is a person; subject is actionable and specific; preview text extends rather than repeats; preview text under 100 chars; emoji used deliberately or deliberately not |
| **Human** | Passes the 1-person test; no jargon or corporate register; reader-focused not sender-focused |
| **Entertain** | Memorable, has a voice, a joke or a hook; not indistinguishable from every other email in the category |
| **Easy** | Exactly one goal; a single CTA repeated; scannable structure; short sentences; adequate white space; CTA points at a campaign-specific page |
| **Transitions** | Section breaks are specified and are something other than a plain horizontal rule |
| **Animate** | At least one considered animation opportunity, subtle, with a static first frame and a file-size note — or a deliberate reasoned decision not to animate |
| **Harmony** | Email promise matches the landing page headline, offer and design; form is short enough for the ask |
| **Valuable** (top tip) | Every section answers "what's in it for me"; benefits, not announcements |

**Overall grade** = the honest average, but **Easy** and **Valuable** are load-bearing.
An email that is funny, animated and beautifully transitioned but has three competing
CTAs and talks only about the sender is a failing email. Say so.
